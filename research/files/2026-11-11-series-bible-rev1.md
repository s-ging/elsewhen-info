# Series bible: alt-history "what if" miniseries (working document)

Status as of November 11, 2026. Channel and series name not decided yet. "Perlangan" is the in-universe name of the nation in Pitch 0 only; it is not the channel or series brand.

## 1. The concept

An anthology of short-form alternate-history miniseries for Reels, Shorts and TikTok (about 1:30 to 2:00 per episode, vertical 1080×1920). Each miniseries changes one historical moment and follows the consequences forward, styled like a strategy game. The tone is analytical and opinionated, rooted in real history plus wishful thinking.

The signature: every episode ends on the real-timeline comparison ("In our timeline, this happened instead"). This separates the channel from AI slop and turns "this is inaccurate" commenters into an audience.

### Credibility rules for every miniseries
1. **One change at a time.** Every divergence traces back to a specific, real moment.
2. **Every invention needs a source.** Either it grows from native tradition (bronze casting, boatbuilding) or arrives through a trade partner. No magic tech jumps.
3. **Nothing is free.** The alternate nation has real problems. A utopia reads as a wish; a country with problems reads as real.
4. **Always end on the real comparison.**

### Content guardrails
- No real living politicians (or their families) as characters or story devices. The earlier "Duterte firewall" idea is permanently dropped. Use fictional dynasties instead.
- Historical figures who died centuries ago (Lapulapu, Humabon, Panday Pira, Hideyoshi) are fair game, shown through character design, not realistic faces.
- Japan sensitivity: Japan is a 400-year ally and peer. The WWII-era villain is a rogue militarist faction with an invented name and flag. Show Japanese characters who oppose it. Keep atrocity imagery off-screen. End on today's warm relationship.
- The southern sultanates (Mindanao) get agency and their own spin-off, never only a buffer or money source.
- No copyrighted game assets, UI, fonts, music or voice lines. Everything is "inspired by."
- Disclose realistic AI-generated or synthetic content where platforms require it.

## 2. Visual and format direction

- **Look references (a study, not copies):** Command & Conquer Generals / Zero Hour (mission-briefing maps, typed location and date stamps), Battle Realms and Warcraft 3 (in-engine letterboxed cutscenes, talking moments in the world), R.U.S.E. (war-table map, labeled zones, forest clusters, zooming from overview to units), Civilization VI (zoomed-in settlements, wonder framing), Cities: Skylines (only for the 2026 payoff).
- **Reference video that works:** an animated short asking whether the University Belt could survive a zombie apocalypse (about 964K views). It works because everything is real and specific: real street maps, campus sizes, student counts, a group-chat cutaway.
- **Building blocks:** tactical map with real coastlines; location plus date stamps that type in (no "Turn" counters); strength and weakness cards; letterboxed cutscenes with subtitles; in-world document cutaways (Hideyoshi's letter, trade ledgers, baybayin messages); vignette and film grain.
- **Character design:** silhouette over facial detail. What separates Kenji from a regular swordsman in Battle Realms is silhouette, clothing, colors and props.
- **Pacing:** aggressive cutting. Boats move at believable speeds on screen; time passes through cuts and date stamps, not fast-forwarded ships.
- **Reels safe zone (1080×1920):** keep text out of the top 250px, bottom 420px, left 70px, and right 55px (widening to 193px in the lower area beside the like and comment buttons).
- **Monetization reality:** Shorts pay little per view, and platforms have been tightening monetization on mass-produced AI content. The protection is a clear voice, real history and a consistent original universe. Facebook Reels may pay better for a Philippine audience. Long-form compilations are worth considering.

## 3. Pitch 0: the Perlangan timeline (lead miniseries, 8 episodes)

### The nation
- **Name:** Perlangan (citizens: Perlanganons). In-universe only. Exonym: Perlandia.
- **Name note:** "perlas" is a Spanish loanword and "Pearl of the Orient" is a colonial-era epithet, so the name technically breaks the anti-colonial naming rule. Decision: keep it (it sounds like a real place) and give it a one-line in-world explanation, or treat the pedantic comments as free engagement. Native alternative root if ever needed: *mutya* (pearl).
- **Alternate names considered:** Lusayas, Puluan, Dagatnon. Rejected: Unluvis (sounds like a modern acronym).
- **Territory:** Luzon, the Visayas and Palawan (Palawan joins the northern union by choice, as it historically chose not to join the Muslim Mindanao autonomous region). The southern sultanates (Maguindanao, Sulu, the Lanao sultanates) stay independent trading partners, a Singapore or Malaysia dynamic.
- **Common language:** a trade Malay, rooted in the Laguna Copperplate Inscription (about 900 CE, mostly Old Malay). This sidesteps "whose language wins."
- **Two poles:** Manila and Cebu as rival capitals, like Tokyo and Osaka.
- **Internal problems (rule 3):** a few merchant-house conglomerates (descended from datu trade houses and Chinese-Perlangan families, like Japan's Mitsui) own ports, rail, banks and malls; activists and rebel groups push back against Manila-centric rule; typhoons.
- **Cultural icon instead of the jeepney:** the painted river ferry, a motorized descendant of the Visayan *paraw*, decorated maximally (sarimanok, okir patterns). Manila stays a canal city because its esteros were never filled in. On land: decorated trams and commuter trains, and decorated kei vans and three-wheelers for the last mile.
- **Walkability, honestly:** losing the jeepney alone doesn't make cities walkable (Bangkok and Jakarta weren't US colonies and are still congested). Perlangan is walkable because it adopts Japan's rail-first model, where private railways built the cities. Real-world hook: Japan is financing Metro Manila's subway and the North-South Commuter Railway today; the alternate timeline is that partnership 400 years earlier.

### Real history the timeline is built on
- 1521: Lapulapu's forces kill Magellan at Mactan (April 27). Humabon's banquet massacre in Cebu (May 1). The crew burns the *Concepción* near Bohol for lack of men. Only the *Victoria* returns to Spain (1522, about 18 men), completing the first circumnavigation.
- Spain's real conquest: Legazpi arrives 1565 (Cebu falls), Manila taken 1570–71.
- Panday Pira: real but sparsely documented Kapampangan cannon-maker tied to Manila. After the conquest he cast cannons for the Spanish.
- Lantaka: bronze swivel cannons (mostly muzzle-loaded).
- 1582: Japanese pirates under a captain the Spanish called Tay Fusa raid Cagayan.
- 1591–92: Hideyoshi's letters demand submission from Spanish Manila. He invades Korea in 1592 and dies in 1598.
- The Ming dynasty banned direct trade with Japan; the Portuguese got rich as middlemen (Macau to Nagasaki).
- Japan closes itself off from the 1630s; the Dutch keep a trading post at Dejima.
- Only Siam stayed independent in 19th-century Southeast Asia, partly as a buffer.
- October 1897: a powerful typhoon hits Samar and Leyte on a track similar to Yolanda's, flattening Tacloban.
- 1939–42: the Philippine Commonwealth's British-built motor torpedo boats ("Q-boats") fight in the Japanese invasion.
- The Sultanate of Sulu held claims to North Borneo, which has oil.

### The 8 episodes
Each about 1:30 to 2:00, structured as missions with several location and date stamps inside.

1. **"The Fleet That Never Went Home"** (Cebu and Mactan, 1521). Mactan, Humabon's banquet, and the coalition finishing what Cebu started by sinking the *Victoria*. No circumnavigation. Butterfly effects: someone else completes it later (probably Drake for England around 1580: "Spain loses the wonder race"); no proof or spice cargo, so Spain's Pacific momentum weakens; Portugal dominates the Spice Islands and becomes the main European rival; the door opens for Pitch C.
2. **"Panday Pira's Guns"** (Cebu to Manila, 1565–1571). Legazpi arrives, karakoa scouts raise the alarm, Manila's cannons hold, and Perlangan is born. Panday Pira never has to cast cannons for Spain.
3. **"The Japan Problem"** (1582–1598). The Cagayan clash with Tay Fusa's pirates, Manila's Japanese merchant quarter, Hideyoshi's 1592 letter in the year he invades Korea, and his death in 1598. Ends on a comments vote: Ending 1, Hideyoshi treats Perlangan as an ally; Ending 2, he threatens it but can't fight on two fronts. Both endings converge on his successor Tokugawa Ieyasu wanting trade.
4. **"The Silver Bridge"** (Manila, Nagasaki and Fujian, 1600–1641). Perlangan becomes the middleman between Japanese silver and Chinese silk, then Japan's one open door when it closes itself off. Perlangan sells deerskins (samurai armor), saltpeter (gunpowder), gold, sappanwood and the prized Luzon jars. Japan sells silver, copper, swords and mass-produced matchlocks.
5. **"Canal City"** (Manila to Edo, 1700s–1868). The esteros thrive and painted paraw ferries appear; merchant houses grow into dynasties; Europe swallows the rest of the region; Perlangan modernizes alongside Meiji Japan. Key line: "The real final boss wasn't Japan. It was the 1800s."
6. **"The Storm That Built a Nation"** (Samar and Leyte, 1897). The great typhoon, then building codes and storm-surge defenses born from grief, the way Japan's codes followed the 1923 and 1995 earthquakes. Ondoy (2009) fits the canal-city thread: maintained waterways instead of clogged drains.
7. **"The Broken Treaty"** (1941–1945). A rogue militarist faction attacks its oldest ally. Torpedo-boat swarms rooted in the Moro and Iranun raiding tradition (not "Vietcong-style"; anti-ship missile boats didn't exist until the 1960s) meet the carriers. Perlangan and the southern sultanates lock down the Borneo straits; Japan's war machine runs out of fuel.
8. **"The Save File"** (Leyte to Manila, 2013–2026). Yolanda hits a prepared coast ("same storm path, 116 years apart"). Then the 2026 tour: archipelago shinkansen with bridges and undersea rail across the San Bernardino and Surigao Straits, Baguio as an alpine cog-railway city, Batanes as a trade gateway to Okinawa, canal-boat Manila, visa-free travel to Japan, and the conglomerates and Manila-centrism activists who show the country still has problems. Final real-timeline comparison.

**Spin-off:** the southern sultanates' own story.

## 4. Other pitches (less developed)

### Pitch A: The 51st State Paradox (comedic one-off, likely viral)
The US fully annexes the Philippines in 1899 and makes it a state.
- **Corrected math:** the House is capped at 435 seats and electoral votes equal House seats plus 2. About 115M Filipinos plus about 335M Americans gives the state roughly 110 House seats and about 112 electoral votes (not 140–150). California shrinks to about 38 seats. 112 of the 270 needed is still over 40% of a win.
- Must acknowledge the Philippine-American War (1899–1902) before statehood.
- Use a presidential year (the brief said 2024 but the setting is 2026).
- Gags: candidates campaigning across Luzon and the Visayas; Jollibee survives only as a beloved regional specialty chain that candidates visit to look "authentic," with a Jollibee-to-McDonald's crossfade.
- Act IV's identity crisis (envying independent ASEAN neighbors) is the emotional core.

### Pitch B: The Imperial Glue (classical era)
What if classical East Asia had its own Alexander or Mansa Musa? Anchor it as a regional story, not Filipino-centric.
- Real seed: the Kingdom of Wu (220–280 CE) sent naval expeditions and envoys to Funan.
- Use period names: Wa (Japan), Funan, later Srivijaya. Tokyo, Malacca and "Indonesia" didn't exist yet. "Austronesian ironworkers," not "Tagalog blacksmiths." Drop "cyberpunk."
- Mansa Musa correction: Mali expanded during his pilgrimage; decline came after his death from succession fights and the rise of Songhai. That is exactly the "brilliant ruler, no clear heir, empire cracks" arc. "Taxed the rich" is a modern projection, and Mali's economy included enslaved people.

### Pitch C: The Uncharted Asian Century
What if the East circumnavigated first? Kris's genuine original question.
- Zheng He sailed west (India, Africa), not east. His ships' "400 feet" length is disputed. The voyages were halted after 1433 and records possibly destroyed, not "burned fleets."
- Crossing the Pacific eastward is genuinely hard (Spain needed decades to find the return route), which is the drama.
- China already knew Europe existed: frame it as reaching Europe by sea.
- Do not cite *1421* by Gavin Menzies (debunked).
- China/India rivalry instead of Spain/Portugal: the strongest real seed is the Chola Empire's naval raid on Srivijaya around 1025. A treaty splitting the oceans, like Tordesillas, with Southeast Asia as the swing power.
- Shared universe: sinking the *Victoria* in Pitch 0 leaves the circumnavigation open for the East.
