## 1. The concept

An anthology of short-form alternate-history miniseries for Reels, Shorts and TikTok (~1:30–2:00 per episode, vertical 1080×1920). Each miniseries changes one structural condition and follows it forward, styled like a strategy-game cutscene. Tone: analytical, opinionated, rooted in real history plus one deliberate wish.

**The signature:** every episode ends by *showing and telling* the real timeline in typed text — no narrator, no lecture.

**Format laws:**
- No narrator, no host.
- No "CHANGE" tags and no stat cards. Show the change, then tell the real history at the end.
- Cutscene language: letterboxed dialogue, typed location/date stamps, in-world documents and objects.
- Every episode is watchable with sound off.

### Credibility rules
1. **One structural change at a time.** Every divergence traces to one real moment or one existing-but-undeveloped network. Everything downstream is consequence, not a new wish.
2. **Every invention needs a source.** Native tradition (bronze casting, lashed-lug boatbuilding, boarding doctrine) or a trade partner. No magic tech jumps.
3. **Nothing is free.** The alternate nation has real problems. A utopia reads as a wish; a country with problems reads as real.
4. **Always end on the real comparison.**

## 2. Content guardrails
- No real living politicians or their families as characters or plot devices. Fictional dynasties only.
- Long-dead historical figures are fair game, shown through silhouette and design, never realistic faces.
- Japan is a 400-year ally and peer. The WWII villain is a rogue militarist faction with an invented name and flag; Japanese opponents of the faction are on screen; atrocity stays off-screen; the arc ends on today's warm relationship.
- The southern sultanates get agency and their own spin-off.
- No copyrighted game assets, UI, fonts, music, or voice lines.
- Disclose synthetic content where platforms require it.

## 3. Visual and format direction
- **References (study, not copies):** C&C Generals/Zero Hour (briefing maps, typed stamps), Battle Realms and Warcraft 3 (letterboxed in-engine cutscenes), R.U.S.E. (war-table zoom, labeled zones), Civilization VI (settlement framing), Cities: Skylines (2026 payoff only).
- **What works:** a zombie-apocalypse short about the University Belt (~964K views) — real street maps, real numbers, one group-chat cutaway.
- **Building blocks:** tactical map on real coastlines; typed date stamps (no turn counters); letterboxed cutscenes with subtitles; in-world documents (Hideyoshi's letter, ledgers, baybayin notes); **recurring physical objects** (the Luzon jar); relay montages; vignette and grain.
- **Character design:** silhouette, clothing, color, props over facial detail.
- **Pacing:** aggressive cuts. Boats move at believable speeds. Time passes through cuts and date stamps, never fast-forwarded ships.
- **Reels safe zone (1080×1920):** top 250px, bottom 420px, left 70px, right 55px (193px in the lower-right beside the buttons) kept clear of text.
- **Monetization:** Shorts pay little; AI mass-production is being throttled. Protection is a clear voice, real history, one original universe. Facebook Reels may pay better for a Philippine audience; long-form compilations are worth planning.

## 5. Pitch 0: the Perlangan timeline

Shot-level plans: [perlangan/storyboard.md](perlangan/storyboard.md).

### The nation
- **Name:** Perlangan (Perlanganons). In-universe only. Exonym: Perlandia.
- **Name note:** "perlas" is a Spanish loanword; the name technically breaks the anti-colonial rule. Keep it, give it a one-line in-world explanation, or treat the pedantry as engagement. Native root if ever needed: *mutya*.
- **Territory:** Luzon, the Visayas, Palawan (joins by choice). The southern sultanates (Maguindanao, Sulu, Lanao) remain independent trading partners.
- **Common language:** trade Malay rooted in the Laguna Copperplate Inscription (~900 CE).
- **Two poles:** Manila and Cebu as rival capitals, equal in prestige — the Visayas earn theirs in 1521.
- **Internal problems:** merchant-house conglomerates owning ports, rail, banks and malls; Manila-centric rule and the activists against it; typhoons.
- **Cultural icon instead of the jeepney:** the painted river ferry, a motorized descendant of the *paraw*. Manila stays a canal city because the esteros were never filled. On land: decorated trams and commuter rail, kei vans and three-wheelers for the last mile.
- **Walkability, honestly:** walkability comes from adopting Japan's rail-first model, where private railways built the cities — not from losing the jeepney. Real hook: Japan finances Metro Manila's subway and the North–South Commuter Railway today; Elsewhen moves that partnership 400 years earlier.

### The founding wish
**A standing Tondo–Visayas maritime compact exists before Spain arrives** — safe passage, shared anchorages, trade in jars, iron and salt. Peer states, not colonies. It is *slow*: word travels within the Visayas in days, but reaches Luzon in months. The compact is renegotiated and hardened in Episode 2.

### Real history the timeline is built on
- 1521: Magellan killed at Mactan (Apr 27). Humabon's banquet massacre (May 1). The crew burns the *Concepción* near Bohol; only the *Victoria* returns to Spain (1522, ~18 men).
- Humabon and Lapulapu were rivals; Humabon sided with Spain at Mactan, then turned on Spain four days later.
- Spain's real conquest: Legazpi 1565 (Cebu), Manila 1570–71.
- Panday Pira: Kapampangan cannon-maker tied to Manila; cast for Spain after the conquest.
- Lantaka: bronze swivel guns, mostly muzzle-loaded.
- Pre-colonial Visayans were long-range raiders (*pangangayaw*); Song records place Visayan raiders off Fujian in the 1100s. Boarding doctrine centered on closing speed, grappling, and the raised *burulan* deck. Lashed-lug hulls flexed on reefs instead of cracking.
- 1582: Japanese pirates under "Tay Fusa" raid Cagayan. 1591–92: Hideyoshi demands submission; invades Korea 1592; dies 1598.
- Ming bans direct trade with Japan; Portuguese profit as middlemen. Japan closes from the 1630s; the Dutch keep Dejima.
- Only Siam stayed independent in 19th-century Southeast Asia, partly as a buffer.
- October 1897: a typhoon on a Yolanda-like track flattens Tacloban.
- 1939–42: the Commonwealth's British-built motor torpedo boats fight the invasion.
- Sulu held claims to North Borneo, which has oil.

### Episode briefs
1. **Victoria's Last** (1521) — the relay, Humabon's double game, Mactan, the banquet, the *Victoria* burned at Bohol. No circumnavigation. Spain's Pacific momentum weakens; Portugal dominates the Spice Islands; the door opens for Pitch C.
2. **Perlangan Rises** (1521 aftermath – 1571) — Tondo hears months late; the compact hardens; four decades of stripping Spanish tech into native hulls; Legazpi meets bronze that came from his own country's ships; Perlangan founded as a compact of peers.
3. **The Sun Sets** (1582–1598) — Tay Fusa's pirates; Manila's Japanese quarter fights alongside Perlangan; Hideyoshi's 1592 letter; his death. **Fork vote: The Sun Burns / The Sun Shines.**
4. **Silver Bridge** (1600–1641) — Perlangan as the middleman between Japanese silver and Chinese silk, then Japan's other open door when it closes.
5. **Canal City** (1700s–1868) — esteros thrive; merchant dynasties; Europe swallows the region; modernization alongside Meiji. Key line: "The real final boss wasn't Japan. It was the 1800s."
6. **Same Path, 116 Years Apart** (1897) — the great typhoon; codes and defenses born from grief; the Ondoy thread. Sets up Ep 8.
7. **The Broken Treaty** (1941–45) — a rogue militarist faction attacks its oldest ally; torpedo-boat swarms from Moro and Iranun raiding tradition; the Borneo straits close; Japan runs out of fuel.
8. **Perlangan, Until We Meet Again** (2013–2026) — Yolanda's track hits a prepared coast; the 2026 tour; the problems that remain; the final real comparison; the series closes. Post-credits: the northbound boat, still moving.
9. **The Sultanate of Sulu** (spin-off) — Sulu as a maritime power with its own treaties, claims, and leverage.

## 6. Other pitches

**Pitch A — The 51st State Paradox** (comedic one-off). The US annexes the Philippines in 1899 and makes it a state. House capped at 435; ~115M Filipinos + ~335M Americans gives roughly 110 House seats and ~112 electoral votes — over 40% of a win. Must acknowledge the Philippine–American War (1899–1902) before statehood. Set in a presidential year. Gags: candidates touring Luzon and the Visayas; Jollibee as a regional chain candidates visit to look authentic; a Jollibee-to-McDonald's crossfade. Emotional core: Act IV's identity crisis, envying independent ASEAN neighbors.

**Pitch B — The Imperial Glue** (classical era). Regional, not Filipino-centric. Real seed: the Kingdom of Wu (220–280 CE) sent naval expeditions to Funan. Use period names: Wa, Funan, later Srivijaya. "Austronesian ironworkers," not "Tagalog blacksmiths." Drop "cyberpunk." Mansa Musa correction: Mali expanded during his pilgrimage; decline followed his death via succession fights and Songhai's rise. "Taxed the rich" is a modern projection; Mali's economy included enslaved people.

**Pitch C — The Uncharted Asian Century.** What if the East circumnavigated first? Zheng He sailed west; the "400-foot" ship figure is disputed; the voyages halted after 1433, records possibly destroyed. Eastward Pacific crossing is genuinely hard — that's the drama. China already knew Europe existed; frame it as reaching Europe by sea. Do not cite Menzies' *1421*. Strongest real seed: the Chola naval raid on Srivijaya (~1025); an ocean-splitting treaty like Tordesillas, with Southeast Asia as the swing power. Shared universe: the *Victoria* never returning leaves the circumnavigation open for the East.
