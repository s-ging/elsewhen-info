## EP 1 — "Victoria's Last"
**Full detail:** [ep01-victorias-last.md](ep01-victorias-last.md)
**Span:** Leyte Gulf, Mar 1521 → Bohol Strait, May 1521
**The one change:** the Visayan sea-lanes already share a warning network, and a standing Tondo–Visayas trade compact exists before Spain arrives.

- Three slow hulls enter Leyte Gulf; a karakoa scout peels away.
- Relay montage: boat → smoke → boat → Mactan shore. A Luzon jar sits on a trade shelf in the background.
- Humabon's court, Apr 7: welcomes Magellan, counts his guns behind the smile.
- Mactan, Apr 27, low tide: reef exposed, armored men wade.
- Battle in silhouette: Magellan falls. Sugbu karakoas arrive "too late," then hold the channel.
- Banquet, May 1: cut before violence — dropped cup, closed door, a Mactan canoe turning away.
- Bohol Strait: karakoas swarm the *Victoria* at dusk; grapple, board, burn. One small boat peels **north**.
- **Object:** the Luzon jar. **Hook:** the northbound boat.
- **Outro (real):** the crew burns the *Concepción*; the *Victoria* reaches Spain in 1522 with eighteen survivors — proof the Pacific could be crossed, and Legazpi follows 44 years later.

## EP 2 — "Perlangan Rises"
**Span:** Tondo, 1521 (aftermath) → Cebu and Manila, 1565–1571
**The one change:** already made in Ep 1. This episode is pure consequence.

- Tondo, months later: the northbound boat finally arrives. The jar changes hands. The compact is renegotiated and hardened — safe passage becomes joint defense.
- Four decades pass in cuts: stripped Toledo steel becomes blades; Spanish lombards get studied; Panday Pira's bronze foundry; lantaka mounts on twin-hulled joangas.
- 1565: Legazpi arrives expecting canoes with bows. Karakoa scouts raise the alarm along the same relay.
- Manila's cannons hold. The bronze that was stripped from the *Victoria* fires back at Spain.
- Founding of Perlangan — a compact of peers, not a conquest.
- **Object:** the jar, now on a Tondo table, then on a Manila war table. **Outro (real):** Cebu falls 1565; Manila taken 1570–71; Panday Pira casts cannons for Spain.

## EP 3 — "The Sun Sets"
**Span:** Cagayan, 1582 → Manila, 1591–92 → Japan, 1598
**Fork episode.**

- 1582: Tay Fusa's Japanese pirates raid Cagayan. Perlangan answers with its own squadrons, not Spain's.
- Manila's Japanese merchant quarter: Japanese residents fight alongside Perlangan against the pirates — the alliance's real foundation.
- 1591–92: Hideyoshi's letter demands submission in the same year he invades Korea.
- Perlangan refuses without insulting him. Trade continues through Nagasaki middlemen.
- 1598: Hideyoshi dies. The sun sets on the invasion era.
- **Fork vote card:** *The Sun Burns* (he threatened, and it cost Perlangan) or *The Sun Shines* (he treated Perlangan as a peer).
- **Outro (real):** Hideyoshi's demands went unanswered; Japan invaded Korea and withdrew after his death; Tokugawa Ieyasu later wanted trade, not tribute.

## EP 4 — "Silver Bridge"
**Span:** Manila, Nagasaki, Fujian, 1600–1641
**The one change:** none new — consequence of Ep 1 and Ep 3.

- Japanese silver, Chinese silk, Perlangan as the middleman the Ming ban created.
- Cargo montage: deerskins for armor, saltpeter, gold, sappanwood, Luzon jars — out. Silver, copper, swords, mass-produced matchlocks — in.
- 1630s: Japan closes. Dejima is the Dutch door; Perlangan is the other one.
- **Outro (real):** Japan's *sakoku* left only the Dutch at Dejima; the Portuguese were expelled in 1639.

## EP 5 — "Canal City"
**Span:** Manila → Edo, 1700s–1868
**The one change:** none new.

- Esteros never filled; painted paraw ferries become the city's icon.
- Merchant houses become dynasties; ports, rail, banks.
- Europe swallows the region around them. Siam survives as a buffer; Perlangan survives as a partner.
- Meiji Japan modernizes; Perlangan modernizes alongside, rail-first.
- **Key line (character, not narrator):** "The real final boss wasn't Japan. It was the 1800s."
- **Outro (real):** the Philippines became a US colony in 1898; only Siam stayed independent in Southeast Asia.

## EP 6 — "Same Path, 116 Years Apart"
**Span:** Samar and Leyte, October 1897 → (callback) 2013
**The one change:** none new.

- The 1897 typhoon flattens Tacloban. Grief becomes code: building standards, storm-surge defenses, mangrove lines, elevated evacuation routes.
- Ondoy thread: maintained waterways instead of clogged drains.
- **Ends forward-looking:** a 2013 storm with the same track, hitting a prepared coast — the payoff is held for Ep 8.
- **Outro (real):** the 1897 typhoon's track resembled Yolanda's; the Philippines had no national building code until decades later.

## EP 7 — "The Broken Treaty"
**Span:** 1941–1945
**The one change:** none new.

- A rogue militarist faction with an invented name and flag attacks Perlangan, its oldest ally.
- Torpedo-boat swarms rooted in Moro and Iranun raiding tradition meet the carriers. No anachronistic missiles.
- Perlangan and the southern sultanates lock the Borneo straits. Japan's war machine runs dry.
- Japanese characters who oppose the faction are on screen. Atrocity stays off-screen.
- **Outro (real):** the Commonwealth's Q-boats fought in the invasion; the war ended in 1945; Japan and the Philippines normalized relations in 1956.

## EP 8 — "Perlangan, Until We Meet Again"
**Span:** Leyte, 2013 → the 2026 tour
**Conclusion.**

- Yolanda's track, 116 years after Ep 6. The coast is ready.
- The 2026 tour: archipelago shinkansen, San Bernardino and Surigao undersea rail, Baguio cog railway, Batanes–Okinawa gateway, canal-boat Manila, visa-free Japan.
- The problems stay: conglomerates, Manila-centrism, activists, typhoons.
- Final real-timeline comparison. Series closes.
- **Post-credits teaser:** the northbound boat from Ep 1, still moving.

## EP 9 — "The Sultanate of Sulu" (spin-off)
**Span:** Sulu and North Borneo, 1400s → present
**Agency, not buffer.**

- Sulu as a maritime power in its own right: treaties, sea-lanes, the North Borneo claims.
- Its relationship with Perlangan: peers and rivals, never a client.
- Oil claims become leverage, not a windfall.
- **Outro (real):** the Sultanate's North Borneo claims persisted into the modern era; Sabah remains disputed.
